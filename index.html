Data smells and data patterns

Good data practice is the same whether your data sits in Delta Lake or three Excel files. The tools and mechanisms may change, but good habits stay the same.

On this site I give names to the things that go wrong (smells) and the habits that prevent them (patterns), with examples in Excel, Power Query and Databricks, and the relevant data engineering terms to search for if you want to read further.

Why this is important

Spreadsheet errors are frequent, silent and unrelated to skill. Most errors are made by very careful people, so the remedy is not to ask people to pay more attention. Instead, add structure and checks.

  • 91%of 54 real-world spreadsheets audited since 1997 contained errors.Panko, 2008
  • 15,841COVID-19 cases missing from UK daily reporting, cut off by a row limit.Public Health England, 2020
  • 1 in 5genomics papers with Excel gene lists had names converted to dates.Ziemann et al., 2016

How I built the pages

  • A name you can say in a meeting. "That's a suitcase cell" is easier than "that violates first normal form".
  • Plain language first. What you see, why it hurts, and what to do about it.
  • Common examples. Excel, Power Query / Power BI, and Databricks (Python and SQL).
  • Search terms. The data engineering vocabulary, so you can find the wider literature.
