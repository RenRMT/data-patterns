A field guide, in plain language

Data smells and data patterns

Good data practice is the same whether the data sits in three Excel files, a Power BI model or Databricks. Only the mechanism changes. I measure data maturity by the habits a team has, not by the tools it bought.

This site names the things that go wrong (smells) and the habits that prevent them (patterns), with examples for each of the three stacks and the data engineering terms to search for if you want to read further.

Why this matters

Spreadsheet errors are frequent, silent and unrelated to skill. The remedy is structure and checks, not asking people to be more careful.

  • 91%of 54 real-world spreadsheets audited since 1997 contained errors.Panko, 2008
  • 15,841COVID-19 cases missing from UK daily reporting, cut off by a row limit.Public Health England, 2020
  • 1 in 5genomics papers with Excel gene lists had names converted to dates.Ziemann et al., 2016

How a page is built

  • A name you can say in a meeting. "That's a suitcase cell" is easier than "that violates first normal form".
  • Plain language first. What you see, why it hurts, what to do.
  • Three stacks. Excel, Power Query / Power BI, and Databricks (Python and SQL). Pick yours with the switch on each page; the choice is remembered.
  • Search terms last. The data engineering vocabulary, so you can find the wider literature.
