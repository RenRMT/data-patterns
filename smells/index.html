Data smells

A data smell is the counterpart of a code smell: a sign that something may be wrong, not proof that it is. A smell you accept on purpose is fine, as long as you write down why.

Fixing at entry is cheaper than fixing in storage, which is cheaper than fixing in the pipeline, which is cheaper than fixing in the report. Each step later multiplies the cost and the number of people who need to know about the fix.

And where a code smell points at a design problem, a data smell usually points at an organisational one. Department names as free text mean that nobody owns the department list. In those cases the fix is a conversation about ownership, not a script.

When it is not a smell

  • Denormalised tables in a reporting layer are a deliberate choice.
  • Wide format in a final product is exactly right.
  • Redundancy for speed is fine, when it is derived and can be refreshed.
  • Received data may be messy. It should be kept unchanged; the cleaning belongs in the next layer.
